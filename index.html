US Automotive Market
Update 2026

The U.S. automotive market is resilient on volume, but affordability remains the central pressure point.

Data cut-off: 22 September 2026 Jan–Aug 2026 + Mid-Sept signals
01The Thesis

A resilient market that is becoming increasingly selective

The new-vehicle market recovered from a weak start to 2026 and established a relatively stable high-16-million sales pace through most of the spring and summer. The seasonally adjusted annual rate moved from 14.8 million in January to 16.8 million in August, with six consecutive months above 16 million. August was the strongest monthly pace of the year.

At the same time, the market is not becoming materially cheaper. The average new-vehicle transaction price reached $50,089 in August, the first month of 2026 above $50,000. Incentive spending fell to 6.5% of ATP, versus 7.2% a year earlier. This means the market is maintaining volume without relying on a broad return to aggressive discounting.

The most important affordability signal is therefore not headline sales, but the structure needed to sustain them.

The typical monthly payment reached $770, while 31.3% of auto loans exceeded 72 months and 57.4% involved negative equity. Negative equity means that the outstanding balance on a vehicle loan is greater than the vehicle's current market value. In practice, this can make the next vehicle purchase harder to finance because part of the old debt has to be carried into the new transaction.

Retail used-vehicle sales increased 3.9% month over month in August, while dealer inventory fell 1.1%. Days' supply tightened from 46 to 44 days. Yet the average used listing price climbed to $27,239, 7% above a year earlier and the highest monthly level since December 2022.

At wholesale level, the direction is now different. The Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index fell from 215.3 in March to 208.2 in August and 206.2 by mid-September. September wholesale values were 0.4% below a year earlier, marking the first negative year-over-year reading of 2026. The used market is therefore showing an increasingly important split between cooling wholesale values and still-elevated retail prices.

Electric vehicles tell a similarly mixed story. New EV sales reached 78,895 units in August, up 2.5% from July but down 46.9% year over year against an exceptionally strong August 2025 comparison. Meanwhile, used EV sales increased 14.7% year over year, and EV inventory moved much closer to the broader internal-combustion market.

The market is therefore not moving in one direction. It is better described as a resilient but increasingly selective market, where volume is being maintained while affordability pressure is moving deeper into financing, vehicle age, inventory composition and consumer choice.

01
Sales are accelerating while inventory is shrinking

August is the clearest expression of this tension. The new-vehicle sales pace increased 3.3% from July, while available inventory fell from 2.73 million to 2.68 million units. Days' supply dropped from 77 to 73 days, its lowest level since April 2025.

This is not a classic oversupply market. At the industry level, supply remains adequate, but the shortage is increasingly concentrated in affordable vehicles. Cars priced at $30,000 or less had only 54 days of supply, compared with more than 90 days for vehicles priced above $60,000.

02
Affordability is improving statistically, but worsening structurally

Cox's Vehicle Affordability Index shows that the number of weeks of median income required to purchase the average new vehicle fell from 35.9 weeks in August 2025 to 35.5 weeks in August 2026. Average Income has therefore grown faster than vehicle prices, which do not mean that median or middle-class income has growth.

But the absolute burden remains high. The average transaction price is now above $50,000, the average monthly payment is $770, and incentives have declined. The market is therefore technically more affordable year over year while still being structurally difficult for many households to access.

03
Credit access is improving, but partly because deal structures are becoming more aggressive

Credit availability reached 105.3 in August, the highest level since November 2015. Approval rates also improved for a fifth consecutive month. But the improvement did not primarily come from cheaper money.

The average contract rate rose to 10.99%. The share of loans longer than 72 months reached a record 31.3%, while 57.4% of loans involved negative equity. Down payments remained at just 13%. These structures reduce the immediate monthly burden or facilitate the transaction, but they can increase total borrowing costs and extend the period during which the borrower owes more than the vehicle is worth.

04
Wholesale used-car prices are falling while retail used-car prices are still rising

The Manheim index reached 215.3 in March, then declined to 208.2 in August and 206.2 by mid-September. At the same time, the average retail used-vehicle listing price reached $27,239 in August, up 7% year over year.

This does not necessarily indicate a contradiction in the data. Wholesale and retail markets operate at different points in the value chain, and retail pricing generally adjusts more slowly.

05
The affordable used-car market is shrinking even as used cars remain the main alternative to new

Edmunds estimates that vehicles below $20,000 represented 55.2% of used sales in Q2 2019, but only 31.8% in Q2 2026. Vehicles priced above $30,000 increased from 16.8% to 35.8%.

The shift is even clearer at the entry level. In Q2 2019, a $10,000-$15,000 budget bought a vehicle averaging 4.7 years old and 58,250 miles. By Q2 2026, the same budget bought a vehicle averaging 8.7 years old and 98,222 miles.

06
EVs are weak in new-car volume but stronger in used-car momentum

August new EV sales fell 46.9% year over year, but used EV sales increased 14.7%. Used EV inventory is benefiting from growing off-lease returns, while new EV inventory is now only two days above the broader ICE+ market.

At the same time, new EV prices have moved closer to the broader market. The average new EV ATP was $54,813 in August, with an EV premium of 9.4%, versus more than 16% a year earlier.

03The New-Vehicle Market

Sales are accelerating as inventory tightens

From a weak start to a sustained high-16M sales pace

The year started weakly. January's SAAR fell to 14.8 million, affected by severe winter weather. February recovered to 15.8 million, March reached 16.3 million, and the market then remained broadly in the high-15 to low-17 million range.

June was subsequently revised upward to approximately 17.0 million, before July moderated to 16.3 million. August then reached 16.8 million, the strongest pace of the year.

August produced a notable supply-demand tension

August sales accelerated while inventory declined for the third consecutive month. Available inventory fell from 2.73 million units in July to 2.68 million in August, while days' supply dropped from 77 to 73. Retail demand therefore continued to absorb inventory rather than allowing it to accumulate.

04Affordability

A $50K vehicle still means a $770 monthly commitment

Affordability is the market's real pressure point.

The average new-vehicle transaction price increased to $50,089 in August, up 0.5% from July and 1.9% year over year. The typical monthly payment reached $770, while the median income required to purchase the average new vehicle rose to 35.5 weeks, slightly above July but below 35.9 weeks a year earlier.

The most interesting feature is the divergence between relative and absolute affordability. Income growth of 3.8% exceeded vehicle price growth of 1.9%, which improved the year-over-year affordability measure. However, the average vehicle still costs more than $50,000 and requires around $770 per month.

05Credit & Deal Structure

Credit is more available, but the structure is getting longer and riskier

Credit access is opening, but through more complex deal structures

The Dealertrack Credit Availability Index increased for the fourth consecutive month to 105.3, its highest level since November 2015. Overall approval reached 73.9%. But the mechanics matter.

The average contract rate reached 10.99%. The share of loans longer than 72 months hit a record 31.3%, and the percentage of loans involving negative equity reached 57.4%.

Negative equity is a particularly important market indicator. It means the outstanding loan balance is higher than the vehicle's current market value. When a customer trades in that vehicle, the difference may have to be financed as part of the next transaction.

06The Used-Vehicle Market

Retail prices keep climbing while wholesale values turn down

Used vehicles remain relatively attractive, but the market is no longer cheap

August used retail sales were estimated at 1.50 million units, up 3.9% from July. Inventory fell 1.1% to 2.13 million vehicles, pushing days' supply down from 46 to 44. Yet the average listing price increased to $27,239, 7% above a year earlier and the highest level since December 2022.

This creates a second affordability tension: used vehicles remain materially cheaper than new vehicles, but the low-price end is becoming increasingly difficult to access. Vehicles below $15,000 represented only 15.1% of used inventory, down from 20.6% a year earlier. Their supply was just 29 days, versus 44 days for the overall used market.

The wholesale market has turned

The Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index rose strongly during the first quarter, peaking at 215.3 in March. It then moved lower. The March peak was driven by a strong spring bounce and tax-refund demand. By August, depreciation had resumed, and by mid-September the index had moved 0.4% below September 2025, the first negative year-over-year reading of 2026.

07The Affordable Used-Car Squeeze

The same budget now buys an older, higher-mileage vehicle

The $15,000 consumer has lost substantial purchasing power

Edmunds provides the clearest long-term illustration. In Q2 2019, vehicles below $20,000 represented 55.2% of used sales. By Q2 2026, that share had fallen to 31.8%. The share above $30,000 increased from 16.8% to 35.8%.

At the $10,000-$15,000 price point, average vehicle age increased from 4.7 to 8.7 years, while mileage rose from 58,250 to 98,222 miles.

08New-Vehicle Price Structure

The U.S. market is still structurally expensive

Prices crossed $50,000 despite consumers moving toward cheaper segments

New-vehicle ATP increased to $50,089 in August, while MSRP reached $51,852. Incentives represented only 6.5% of ATP, down from 7.2% one year earlier. The market is still dominated by high-value body types, but consumers are increasingly shifting toward lower-priced segments.

~65% of August sales came from the five highest-volume segments. The $50K average is a structural result of the U.S. vehicle mix rather than simply a pricing decision by individual manufacturers. Source: Kelley Blue Book / Cox Automotive.
